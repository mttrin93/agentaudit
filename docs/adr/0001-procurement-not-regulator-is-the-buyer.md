# Procurement, not a regulator, is the buyer

AgentAudit maps findings to EU AI Act Articles 15, 14, 12 and 50, which invites a reader to assume a compliance product. It is not one. Under Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (published in the OJ 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026), Articles 12, 14 and 15 bind high-risk systems only and are not enforceable until 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems or 2 August 2028 for Annex I; Article 50 is the sole article in force today. And where the high-risk articles do apply, conformity assessment for Annex III points 2 to 8 runs through Annex VI internal control: the provider assesses its own system and no notified body reads the file.

**Decision.** The buyer is enterprise procurement and insurer diligence. The Act supplies the rubric for *what to test*; it is not the reason to buy. The displaced default is the hand-filled security questionnaire — a document a human completes from recollection, under commercial pressure, for a customer who cannot verify any of it.

## Considered options

- **Provider of an Annex III high-risk agent, pre-compliance framing.** Honest, but ties demand to a 2027 date and to a classification most agents do not meet.
- **GPAI providers with systemic risk under Article 55**, where the Code of Practice does mandate adversarial testing before deployment, before each major update and at least annually. Rejected: that is a handful of frontier labs, not five findable users.
- **Rejected framing: "fires the consultant's first pass."** Under Annex VI there is no notified body for Annex III points 2 to 8, so no external auditor exists to displace. The claim failed on the facts.

## Consequences

No claim of conformity and no badge — see [ADR-0005](./0005-no-composite-risk-score.md). The report format is an open question that must be answered by real users during recruitment, not assumed: procurement may want SOC 2, ISO 42001 or its own questionnaire template rather than an Act-mapped document. Ask in the first user conversation.
