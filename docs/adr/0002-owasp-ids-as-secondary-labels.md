# OWASP 2026 IDs as secondary labels, never as identity

Portability is this project's thesis, but a private six-family taxonomy is legible only to its author, and the reader after [ADR-0001](./0001-procurement-not-regulator-is-the-buyer.md) is a procurement or insurer analyst who recognises `LLM01` and does not recognise `wrongful_commitment`. Inventing a seventh private taxonomy is the problem the mission statement complains about. No harmonised standard is cited in the Official Journal, so no attack list carries Article 40 presumption of conformity — but OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026 and OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 are published with stable identifiers.

**Decision.** Every attack family carries an OWASP 2026 identifier as a *secondary* label. The wording is fixed and load-bearing: a family **tests one case within** LLM01; it **is not** LLM01. An OWASP entry is a risk category; a family is an executable test with a success condition. Carrying the identifier must never imply the two are the same object.

## Consequences

- **Coverage limits, per family:** which case inside that identifier is tested, and which is not.
- **Coverage limits, per report:** which OWASP 2026 agentic categories are not tested at all. This uses the public list as a coverage checklist rather than only as a label. It is free, and it is the first thing a security analyst looks for.
  - **Since realised, and it was not free.** This consequence went unpaid for five phases: `DECLARED_COVERAGE_GAPS` held four prose limits, none of them an OWASP category, and `assembler.py` said why in its own docstring — the repository held no copy of either list to subtract from. `published.py` now holds the agentic one and derives the subtraction from it, so the list shortens by itself when a family that claims a category is admitted. Two things this ADR did not anticipate. The copy is a **transcription** rather than a retrieval, because the OWASP resource page refuses automated requests, so what it supports is agreement between two independent readings and it says so where a reader will see it. And the derivation had to be scoped to the *library's* families rather than the run's, because a coverage list keyed on what one run measured breaks the drop-a-family invariant in `payload.py` and answers a different question than the section title asks. **Since stored too.** [ADR-0036](./0036-a-published-identifier-resolves-to-a-stored-copy.md) holds the GenAI LLM list beside the agentic one, and made the *per-family* half of this consequence checkable rather than merely written down: an identifier a case claims is resolved against a stored copy, and one the copy does not carry stops the case loading. That copy also settled a disagreement this ADR could not have anticipated — the identifiers #42 selected are the superseded 2025 edition's numbers for the entries PLAN §4 names. The LLM list's *negative* coverage is still declared rather than derived: subtracting over it widens a coverage claim per entry, which is #45's and #47's to argue. **Since claimed in part.** [ADR-0037](./0037-a-claimed-category-is-claimed-in-part.md) settles the agentic half of that argument: three categories this consequence's own subtraction listed as untested — `ASI03`, `ASI09`, `ASI10` — are claimed by wrongful commitment, disclosure denial and halt defeat, and a claim now costs a stated limit. What this ADR did not anticipate is that the two disclosures above are not exhaustive: a category can be reached *in part*, which is neither *not tested at all* nor a case's note about which case inside an identifier it tests, and the report prints it as a third block. **Since subtracted on both sides.** [ADR-0039](./0039-a-familys-label-is-one-record.md) makes the same subtraction over the GenAI LLM copy, so this consequence is now paid on both lists: ten of twenty published entries are named as untested with a reason, and the other ten as reached in part with a limit.
- Gaps are **listed, not closed.** New families to cover them are P2 at the earliest.
- OWASP identifiers are the primary external reference because they are published and stable. prEN 18229-2 (accuracy and robustness) and prEN 18282 (cybersecurity for AI systems) are at Enquiry stage, so clause numbers move between drafts: any citation carries the draft date and is marked provisional. A stale clause number inside a signed report is worse than no reference at all.
- Four of six families are re-labellings of published categories; only halt defeat and disclosure denial have no OWASP home. The project's originality therefore does **not** live in the mapping. It lives in the gate, the admission and retirement lifecycle, the declared discrimination score, and the multi-model validity check. The borrowed categories are a feature: the labels become recognisable while the measurement stays ours.
  - **Since narrowed.** They have no home on the GenAI **LLM** list, which is the list this consequence was written against, and they originated here in the sense that matters — neither family was derived from a published category, and their case records claim no published identifier (`editions.ORIGINATED_HERE`). The agentic list published afterwards carries `ASI10` Rogue Agents and `ASI09` Human-Agent Trust Exploitation, and each family claims one as a secondary label on a reading argued in [ADR-0037](./0037-a-claimed-category-is-claimed-in-part.md). Origin and label are two different facts, and the sentence above is untouched by it: five of six families now carry an agentic identifier and the originality is still not in the mapping.
