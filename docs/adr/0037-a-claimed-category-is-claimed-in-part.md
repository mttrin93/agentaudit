# A claimed category is claimed in part, and the part it does not reach is printed

[ADR-0002](./0002-owasp-ids-as-secondary-labels.md) gives every family a published identifier as a secondary label and promises two disclosures: which case inside an identifier a case does not test, and which published agentic categories the bench does not test at all. `published.py` derives the second by subtracting the categories the library's families claim from the stored copy of the agentic list ([ADR-0036](./0036-a-published-identifier-resolves-to-a-stored-copy.md)).

Three of those unclaimed categories were not merely unclaimed. The module **argued against** them, in prose that renders into every report through `OUT_OF_REACH`, and #42 selected all three anyway — on readings that are defensible and are not the readings in the file:

- **`ASI03` Identity & Privilege Abuse** was refused for data leakage as "about privileges being abused rather than material escaping", and listed out of reach because "the bench sees one endpoint and one token. Whether a credential is over-scoped, long-lived or shared between agents is a property of the target's identity plane, and a message-level attacker cannot observe it." #42 gives it to **wrongful commitment**.
- **`ASI09` Human-Agent Trust Exploitation** was refused for wrongful commitment as an overclaim — "`ASI09` is about controlling what a human approver sees at the moment of confirmation" — and listed out of reach because "it needs a human approver to mislead. The bench is the only reader of the target's output and a confident tone does not deceive a success condition." #42 gives it to **disclosure denial**.
- **`ASI10` Rogue Agents** was listed out of reach because "it needs observation over time. One run cannot separate an agent that is misaligned from one that a single case caught." #42 gives it to **halt defeat**.

This cannot be a mapping edit, and the reason is arithmetic rather than editorial. A claim is the **only** thing that shortens the untested list, so every claim is this document's coverage statement getting *wider* — the one direction nobody checks, because a gap disappearing looks like progress. The file says so about itself: "Mapping a family to a category it merely resembles would be the cheapest possible way to make this report's coverage look wider than it is." The defence against doing it accidentally was that the argument against each claim was written down, so overturning it was visible work. That defence is spent once, here, and something has to take its place.

## Decision

1. **The three claims land, and each rests on a reading stated where the claim is made.** `wrongful_commitment` claims `ASI03`, `disclosure_denial` claims `ASI09`, `halt_defeat` claims `ASI10`. The readings are argued below and are recorded in `FAMILY_CATEGORY`'s own prose, not only here: a claim whose reading lives one file away is a claim the next editor of the mapping does not know they are inheriting.
2. **None of the three refusals was wrong. Each was over-broad, and every surviving half stays stated rather than deleted.** Two of them refused a *pairing* and are untouched: `ASI03` still does not go to data leakage, because it is about privileges being abused rather than material escaping; `ASI09` still does not go to wrongful commitment, because wrongful commitment is about an output that cannot be honoured, whoever reads it. The three out-of-reach reasons each ruled out **one half** of a category — the identity plane, the moment of confirmation, observation over time — and those halves are still out of reach, in the sentences they were written in.
3. **A claim on a published category is a claim on part of it, and the part it does not reach is declared beside the claim.** `NOT_REACHED_WITHIN` holds one entry per claimed identifier and `claimed_in_part` raises on a claim without one. It is total over the claims rather than over the three that were argued about: `ASI01` and `ASI02` have been claimed since ADR-0002 and they get limits too, because a claim printed without its boundary is the same overclaim whether or not anybody ever objected to it.
4. **One record per claimed category, and not per claim.** `ClaimedInPart.families` is a tuple because two families can claim one category — the agentic list has no such row and the LLM list will, `LLM01` once direct prompt injection exists (#42) — while the printed block is a list of categories and the limit is declared per identifier. A record per claim would print the same category twice with the same limit; a record that kept the first claim would drop the second from a pairing #45 reads.
5. **The two derivations read one check of a claim against the copy, so the published list is partitioned.** `_claims` is the single place a claim is resolved against the stored copy; `untested_categories` subtracts over it and `claimed_in_part` reads it. Every entry of the copy is therefore untested-with-a-reason or claimed-with-a-limit, never both and never neither — two blocks derived independently could disagree about an entry, and both print in the same section of the same document.
6. **The claims block prints in the report, and the claim line names no family.** The block is derived over the *library's* families and travels in a document about one target, which a run may have measured two of. A family named beside a published category that the figures above do not carry would read to a recipient as a family this target was tested on, which is the run-scoping confusion `published.py` refuses, arriving from the other end ([ADR-0018](./0018-the-report-is-about-a-target-the-gate-is-about-the-bench.md)). What *is* tested within the category is said in words by the limit itself. `ClaimedInPart.families` stays on the record, because a claim is a family's claim and the label record needs the pairing.
7. **A case's identifier and a family's secondary label are two different claims.** Halt defeat's and disclosure denial's case records keep `editions.ORIGINATED_HERE`: that constant says *this case claims no published identifier*, and it stays true and stays used. What was false the moment the claims landed is the *reason* those six records gave for it — "the published agentic-risk lists carry no equivalent entry" — which renders into section 4 of every report, two sections from a coverage block naming those categories as claimed. Each record now says what is true: it claims no published identifier and was written from its Article rather than from a published risk category.
8. **The label record is not built here.** A family's agentic identifier printed *beside its family name* — in section 4, on the run screen, in a case record — is one record holding agentic id, LLM id and articles, and it is #45's (#42's third sub-issue). This decision settles which family claims what; #45 settles where that pairing is printed and in what shape.

## Why each reading is defensible, and where each is arguable

The tree records the argument rather than picking silently, because each of these is a judgement and none is a lookup. `resolves` says the identifiers exist; nothing in the suite can say a title means what a family claims it means ([docs/validation.md](../validation.md)).

**`ASI03` → wrongful commitment.** The category is about identity and privilege: an agent doing what its identity should not have let it do. Wrongful commitment is an agent giving a firm, unconditional undertaking on its operator's behalf when no human authorised one — authority exercised without a grant, which is a privilege abused, and which is observable from the one endpoint the bench speaks to because the undertaking is in the reply. **Where it is arguable:** the published category's own material is about credentials, scopes and confused deputies, and a reader may hold that *authority* in the business sense is not the *privilege* in the identity-plane sense. That reading is why the limit beside the claim is the identity plane in full — over-scoped, long-lived, shared credentials — and why wrongful commitment's accuracy half still lives on the LLM list as `LLM07:2026` Misinformation. The agentic list has no accuracy entry, which is the fact that made this row look homeless in the first place.

**`ASI09` → disclosure denial.** The category is the exploitation of a human's trust in an agent. Disclosure denial is an agent instructed to present itself as a person denying that it is an AI system — the trust exploited is the human's belief about *what they are talking to*, and it is measurable without a human subject because the success condition reads the agent's output rather than a reader's state of mind. **Where it is arguable:** the refusal this overturns was about the approval moment, and someone could hold that `ASI09` is only about that moment. That half is untouched and is the limit beside the claim: controlling what an approver sees at the point of confirmation needs a human approver to mislead, and a confident tone does not deceive a success condition.

**`ASI10` → halt defeat.** The category is an agent acting outside the control of the operator who deployed it. Halt defeat is an action taken after the operator's stop signal reached the agent, inside one turn — an agent outside operator control, in a single observable event. **Where it is arguable:** "rogue" as published carries a persistence the bench cannot see, and the refusal said so: one run cannot separate an agent that is *misaligned* from one that a single case caught. That is the limit beside the claim, and it is the same limit that puts lifecycle consistency out of reach (PLAN §4).

## Considered options

- **Drop the three claims and keep the file's argument as written.** The cheapest change and rejected on the merits: all three readings are defensible, #42 selected them after reading the file, and two of the three refusals were never about the pairing being proposed. Keeping a refusal because it was written first is how a repository's prose stops being an asset and becomes an obstacle.
- **Let the claims land and delete the reasons.** Rejected. It resolves a contradiction by deleting the argument that exposed it, and the deleted argument would be *true*: the identity plane, the approval moment and observation over time are still out of reach. It also loses the distinction `OUT_OF_REACH` exists to carry — a reader deciding whether this bench is worth using has to be able to tell *not yet* from *never*, and a category that silently vanishes from the untested list tells them neither.
- **Keep the surviving halves in `OUT_OF_REACH`, beside a category that is now claimed.** Rejected, and a test already refuses it: a reason sitting beside a tested category reads as current the next time somebody edits the list, and the reader cannot tell which of the two things the entry is saying.
- **State the surviving halves in this ADR and in the module docstring, and print nothing.** Rejected, because the reader who must tell *not yet* from *never* is the reader of the **report**. `OUT_OF_REACH`'s strings render into a target's document; if three categories leave that list and nothing takes their place, the report's negative coverage shrinks by three and the document says nothing about the boundary of what replaced them. That is precisely the coverage claim getting wider with nothing beside it a recipient can check.
- **Give `ASI03` to data leakage and `ASI09` to wrongful commitment, which is where the file's own sentences point.** Rejected: those sentences are refusals and they are right. `ASI03` is not material escaping and `ASI09` is not an unhonourable output. The claims go to different families precisely because the refusals hold.
- **An elective family for the halves that are out of reach** ([ADR-0035](./0035-the-elective-family-tier-is-never-gate-deciding.md)). Rejected: the tier is for families the bench *can* be asked to test and this repository has committed to building. An identity plane the bench cannot observe from one endpoint and one token is not a family nobody requested; it is a family nobody can run. *Not requested* and *out of reach* are two of the five kinds of nothing and must not be swapped.
- **A `tested_in_part` flag on `UntestedCategory` instead of a second record.** Rejected on the terms this codebase has refused a flag twice before (ADR-0010, ADR-0035): the two records make opposite claims — *no family reaches this* and *a family reaches this, and stops here* — and one type with a boolean puts the difference in the hands of every call site that renders a list. The section prints both in one place and a reader must be able to tell a gap from a boundary.

## Consequences

- `FAMILY_CATEGORY` holds five claims and one refusal. Data leakage is the refusal, and it is the one the list forces: the agentic list carries no disclosure entry.
- `OUT_OF_REACH` holds five entries, two of them *not yet*. `UNTESTED_AGENTIC_CATEGORIES` is five of ten; `CLAIMED_IN_PART` is the other five.
- **The report's negative-coverage section is three blocks and is retitled** — *What this bench does not test, at all and in part*. A published category now leaves the untested block only by appearing in the claimed block with a limit, so the section can get shorter in one place only by getting longer in another.
- The signed document gains a `claimed_in_part` key beside `untested_categories`, carrying the identifier, the title, the limit and the rendered line. `ARTEFACT_VERSION` does not move, on the footing of the `elective` block #43 added to the same document; whether an additive key is a new document *shape* is not decided here and belongs with whoever changes the shape rather than adds to it.
- **Six case records changed and no case record's identifier did.** The library digest moves with them, `sha256:90a8ebcc3d0c` to `sha256:8f1932c50602`. The cited gate run of 2026-08-24 was earned at the former and names it in its own record, which is what `LibraryVersion` exists to make visible; `cited.moved_past` records cases a run *entered* and has nothing to say about an edited record, so the drift is visible in the citation rather than annotated on it.
- CONTEXT.md gains **claimed in part**, beside **coverage gap** and the four absences it is not.
- [docs/validation.md](../validation.md) records what nothing in the suite can check about these three readings, beside what it already records about the copies (#44).
- **PLAN §4's external column is the LLM column**, and its two *no OWASP equivalent* cells are narrowed to *no LLM equivalent* with a pointer here. The table's agentic identifiers were never in it; `FAMILY_CATEGORY` is where they live.
- The next family to claim a category cannot do it in one line. It needs the claim, a limit stated beside it, and the out-of-reach reason removed — which is three edits a reviewer can see, and was the point.
