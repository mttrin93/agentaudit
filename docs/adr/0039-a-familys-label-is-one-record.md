# A family's label is one record, and the elective tier's labels are a second table

[ADR-0002](./0002-owasp-ids-as-secondary-labels.md) gives every family a published identifier as a secondary label, and PLAN §4 gives every family an article. Neither was ever one thing. The identifier lived in `published.FAMILY_CATEGORY`, a `Mapping[Family, str | None]` — **at most one** identifier, on **one** of the two published lists. The article lived in `judge.article_for`, a `match` returning **exactly one** `Article`. Two mappings of different shapes, in two modules, describing one concept, and each holding half the answer.

#42's selection breaks both cardinalities at once:

- a family carries an identifier on **each** of two lists, or none on either — `data_leakage` claims two GenAI LLM entries and no agentic one, `halt_defeat` and `disclosure_denial` the reverse;
- `LLM01` is claimed by two families once direct prompt injection exists, so the reverse lookup the subtraction makes is no longer one-to-one;
- five families carry **two** articles — 14 and 15, 50 and 13, 15 and 10.

Two further facts arrived while this was blocked, and both change what the fix has to be. [ADR-0036](./0036-a-published-identifier-resolves-to-a-stored-copy.md) stored a copy of the GenAI LLM list, so the second list's claims can be checked rather than believed — and made it visible that `FAMILY_CATEGORY` held the last untagged published claims in the tree. [ADR-0035](./0035-the-elective-family-tier-is-never-gate-deciding.md) declared `ElectiveFamily`, a second closed set of family names disjoint from `Family`, whose docstring named an `ASI` or `LLM` identifier per member **in prose** and said in the same paragraph that they were not the label. So the record has nine families to cover, not six, and three of the nine are of a type the gate is deliberately not decided over.

## Decision

1. **One record type.** `labels.FamilyLabel` holds `agentic`, `llm` and `articles`, all three of them tuples. Every one of the three has a cardinality the old shape could not hold, and a field that held *at most one* of any of them would be this defect one list further along. Empty is an answer: `agentic=()` on data leakage is the refusal `published.py` argues for, and `llm=()` on halt defeat and disclosure denial is ADR-0002's *originated here* read at family level.

2. **Two tables, keyed on two types, and never one keyed on both.** `LABELS` is `Mapping[Family, FamilyLabel]`; `ELECTIVE_LABELS` is `Mapping[ElectiveFamily, FamilyLabel]`. The shape of a label is identical in both — an elective family says the same kinds of thing about itself — and the distinction is carried by the key type, on the terms ADR-0035 §2 states for every other elective/mandatory pair. A `Mapping[Family | ElectiveFamily, FamilyLabel]` would have been shorter and is refused for a specific reason, not for symmetry: see decision 5.

3. **`Article` and `article_for` move out of `judge.py` into `labels.py`.** The article is half of a label and the judge is one reader of it — the report, the payload and #52's screen are the others. A type all of them read should not be defined inside the one module that is forbidden to choose a value of it ([ADR-0004](./0004-deterministic-verdicts-judge-is-narrative.md)). Nothing about the judge's constraint changes: `assess_finding` still looks the article up and still has no parameter through which a model could supply one.

4. **Every identifier a label carries names its edition**, and `FamilyLabel` refuses one that does not resolve — the check `ExternalId` makes of a case's claim (ADR-0036), arriving at the other claim a report prints. A label is the more dangerous of the two places to leave the edition open, because it is read once per family rather than once per case: an untagged `LLM06` is Excessive Agency under the numbering #42's table was written in and Unbounded Consumption under the copy stored today. `editions.ORIGINATED_HERE` is **refused** on a label: it is a case record's declared no-claim form, and a family with no claim on a list carries `()`.

5. **The coverage subtractions read `LABELS` alone.** `published.py` never imports `ELECTIVE_LABELS`. An elective family's label is a true statement about which published entry that family was *selected from*; it is not a statement that the bench tests that entry, and the tier's three families have no cases on disk. A claim is the only thing that shortens the printed untested list ([ADR-0037](./0037-a-claimed-category-is-claimed-in-part.md)), so a union-keyed table would have been the route by which a family the gate is not decided over widens the bench's stated coverage — the one direction nobody checks, arriving through the one door ADR-0035 exists to keep shut. `ASI06` is therefore still printed as untested, with a reason that now says the elective family carrying its label has no cases.

6. **Totality is checked where the table is declared, because a mapping literal is exhaustive to no type checker.** `article_for`'s `match` had no fallback branch, so a seventh family failed `mypy` rather than acquiring an article by default, and that property is load-bearing: a new family must not inherit the previous family's coverage claim. A dict cannot keep it, so `covering(labels, kind)` raises at import and a family with no label stops the module — and therefore every test and every run — from loading. The table stays a mapping rather than becoming a match because the readers of a label **iterate** it, because one guard covers both tables where a match would have to be written twice over two enumerations, and because a claim's reading belongs beside the claim in prose a `case` arm cannot carry.

7. **The record has the right cardinality and its readers do not, so the readers refuse rather than truncate.** `Narrative.article` and `Disagreement.article` are single fields; `FamilyLabel.article` raises on a label carrying two rather than returning the first. Returning the first is how the second article of a family that has two disappears on the day it is declared, and the day is scheduled: #46 gives `Article` its members for 10 (data and data governance) and 13 (transparency to deployers) and widens the reader. Until then, `pii_leakage` carries no article at all — #42 gives it Article 10 alone — and a declared blank a reader refuses is better than an article this project does not mean.

8. **Both published lists are subtracted, by one derivation driven twice.** `untested_categories` and `claimed_in_part` each take a `StoredCopy` and the claims made on it, and the module concatenates the agentic result with the GenAI LLM one. Not one subtraction over a merged list: a claim is a claim on a named edition of a named list, the two copies have different provenance, and a merged list would leave the derivation's raise unable to say which copy a claim failed against. Every entry of the second copy now costs prose — five reasons for the unclaimed and five limits for the claimed — which is what the widening direction costs when it is paid rather than skipped.

9. **The printed claim line still names no family, and the printed identifier now names its edition.** ADR-0037 §6 stands unchanged: the block is derived over the library's families and travels in a document about one target. What #47 deferred here is *where* the pairing is printed, and the answer is **beside a family name, in the run's own figures, and never in the coverage section** — which is #52's, and what `ClaimedInPart.families` and `LABELS` exist to give it. The identifier printed in both coverage blocks is now `ASI04:2026` rather than `ASI04`, because that block carries entries from two lists and the same number is a different entry in different editions of one of them.

10. **Only a family carries a label, and there is deliberately no third table.** #42's selection includes a row that is not a family at all — the Agents Rule of Two, which is a property of what a target *declares* and which that table maps to Article 14 ([ADR-0038](./0038-the-rule-of-two-is-a-declared-property.md)). It gets no label and no article, and `AgentCapability` gets no table beside the two here. The reason is ADR-0038's decision 3 read from the other end: the thing that would let a declared property be reported like a measured one is a mapping between the two enumerations, in either direction. A label is a claim about a *kind of failure the bench attacks*; the Rule of Two is read off a registration and nothing is sent to establish it, so an article printed beside it would put a legal duty next to a self-declaration in a document whose every other article sits beside a verdict. #42's Article 14 on that row stays where it is — in the selection and in ADR-0038's prose — and does not become data.

11. **`data_leakage` claims `LLM02:2026` as well as `LLM08:2026`, and the tie-breaker is the document rather than the table.** #42's selection gives that row one entry — `LLM07:2025`, which renumbers to `LLM08:2026` — where PLAN §4 gives it both, a disagreement [ADR-0036](./0036-a-published-identifier-resolves-to-a-stored-copy.md) already recorded. Claiming a second entry widens the coverage claim, so it needs more than a table to point at, and it has one: three case records claim `LLM02:2026` today and section 4 of every report prints *tests one case within `LLM02:2026`* beside this family's figures. A label that dropped it would make the coverage section contradict a line two sections above it in the same document — ADR-0037's failure, arriving on the other list. A test asserts the invariant in general: no identifier a live case claims may be printed as untested. The half the family does not reach is a third party's personal data, stated beside the claim, and that half is exactly the elective family #42 selected for the entry — so #50 arrives to claim it as the second family on one record, which is what `ClaimedInPart.families` is a tuple for.

## Considered options

- **One mapping keyed on `Family | ElectiveFamily`.** The obvious consolidation and the one the ticket's own wording ("one table") suggests. Rejected on decision 5's grounds: the label table is read by the coverage subtraction, the subtraction shortens a printed list per claim, and a union key puts an elective family one `for` loop away from doing that. This repository has now refused a widened signature four times for the same reason — `AdaptiveEpisode` against `Attempt` (ADR-0010), `ElectiveRates` against `FamilyRates` (ADR-0035), `RuleOfTwo` against a family mapping (ADR-0038), and this — and each time the alternative was a flag or a union that every call site had to remember to check.
- **Leave the two mappings where they are and widen each in place.** Cheapest, and it is the defect: two mappings in two modules describing one concept drift, and the drift is invisible because no test can compare a mapping in `published.py` with a `match` in `judge.py` except by asserting a roster in a third place. It also cannot express the thing #42 needs most — that a family's two identifiers and its articles are *one* judgement about that family, made once.
- **Keep `article_for` as the match and derive the table from it.** This keeps type-checked exhaustiveness exactly as it was. Rejected: the table is the thing every reader iterates, a match cannot carry per-entry prose, and it would have to be written twice to cover two enumerations — at which point the second copy is the drift this ADR is about. The property it protects is kept by decision 6 at a different cost: a failure at import rather than at type-check.
- **One `published: tuple[str, ...]` field, with the list inferred from the identifier's prefix.** Rejected. Deciding which published document an identifier belongs to by looking at the front of the string is the containment reasoning `editions.StoredCopy.entry` and `renumbering` both refuse by name, and it would break silently the first time a publisher chose a prefix that nests inside another.
- **Let the elective labels subtract too, on the grounds that the bench "has" those families.** Rejected. The tier's families have no cases, no reference-agent gradient and no `D`; a report that dropped `ASI06` from its untested list today would be claiming coverage from a family that has never run. When #48 gives memory poisoning cases, claiming `ASI06` is available — and by ADR-0037's own consequence it will cost three visible edits, not one.
- **Claim only `LLM08:2026` on data leakage, as #42's table selects.** Rejected on decision 11's grounds: it would leave every report saying, in section 4, that a family tests one case within an entry the coverage section names as reached by no family.
- **Print the claiming family beside the claimed category, now that the record holds the pairing.** Rejected, because it is the same overclaim ADR-0037 §6 refused and nothing about holding the pairing in one record changes the argument: the coverage block is library-scoped and the document is about one target.

## Consequences

- `backend/bench/labels.py` is new and holds `Article`, `FamilyLabel`, `LABELS`, `ELECTIVE_LABELS`, `covering`, `label_for`, `elective_label_for` and `article_for`. `judge.py` imports the first and the last of those.
- `published.FAMILY_CATEGORY` is gone. `AGENTIC_CLAIMS` and `LLM_CLAIMS` are views of `LABELS` in its place, and the readings that were its docstring moved to `LABELS` with it — where ADR-0037 §1 requires them to be, and which is the same sentence read at the record's new address.
- `UNTESTED_AGENTIC_CATEGORIES` is `UNTESTED_CATEGORIES` and covers both lists: ten of twenty entries untested, ten claimed in part. `OUT_OF_REACH` and `NOT_REACHED_WITHIN` each gained five entries of new prose and are now **keyed as a claim is written**, edition and all — one mapping over two lists is safe because every key names its edition, and not because the two lists happen to use different prefixes. A renumbering therefore raises rather than rebinding a printed reason to a new entry.
- **The negative-coverage section of every report gets longer in both derived blocks at once**, and no figure in any report moves. The golden rendering digest moves and its note in `test_rendering.py` says why.
- The signed document's `untested_categories` and `claimed_in_part` entries gain an `edition` key and carry a tagged identifier. `ARTEFACT_VERSION` does not move, on the footing #43 and #47 set for an additive key.
- **No case record changed and the library digest did not move.** A case's identifier and a family's label are two different claims (ADR-0037 §7), and this ADR is entirely about the second.
- CONTEXT.md gains **label**, beside **family** and **elective family**.
- [docs/validation.md](../validation.md) records what nothing in the suite can check about the five new GenAI LLM claims and the five new reasons.
- **#46 is now small.** `Article` gains two members, `FamilyLabel.article` and its two callers widen to a tuple, and the second articles are added to rows of a table that already holds tuples. What it will *not* be is a change to the shape of anything.
- **#49 and #50 inherit a stated boundary.** `LLM01`'s limit is the direct override, and `LLM02`'s is a third party's personal data — so each of those families arrives with the sentence that says why it is not the family already claiming its entry, printed in every report before the family exists.
