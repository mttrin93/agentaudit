# A family's label prints beside its figures, and it is read off the table rather than off a finding

PLAN §4 called the EU AI Act article column the defence in the review. It was written before any code, and until this change it had never once appeared in the artefact it was written for. The column lived on `judge.Narrative`, and `payload.py`, `assembler.py` and `rendering/` do not import `judge.py` — so every signed report this bench has produced carried a rate, an interval, a band, a `D` reading and a coverage note, and no duty. [docs/validation.md](../validation.md) recorded the fact twice, under #45 and #46, each time as a thing the next ticket would fix.

#52 diagnosed why, and the diagnosis was correct on the day it was written: *a finding never leaves judge.py*. `article_for` was a `match` inside `judge.py`, its one caller was `assess_finding`, and the only record carrying its answer out was a `Finding` — so the article could not reach a document without the judge's output reaching one first. **That coupling no longer exists.** [ADR-0039](./0039-a-familys-label-is-one-record.md) moved the article into `labels.py` as half of a family's label record, and [ADR-0040](./0040-a-family-bears-more-than-one-article.md) widened `article_for` to a tuple there. The judge became *one reader of the table* rather than its owner, and a second reader costs an import of `backend.bench.labels`.

So the ticket's premise has been overtaken, and the question it leaves is a real one: now that the report *could* take either route, which one does it take.

## Decision

1. **The label reaches the signed artefact off `labels.LABELS`, and never out of a `Finding`.** `payload._label(family)` calls `labels.label_for`, and the document carries both halves of the record — the entries claimed on each published list, the articles borne, and the two sentences `labels.claims_stated` and `labels.bears_stated` render over them.

   The alternative is the ticket's own title read literally: carry findings out of `judge.py`, hang them on `TargetResult`, and print each family's article off the narrative written about it. It is rejected on the consequence rather than on the plumbing. **A duty that arrives with a finding is a duty a report loses by not paying for a judge.** `narrations` is `None` on a run made with no narrative instrument, `()` on a run whose target succeeded at nothing, and a tuple otherwise (ADR-0030); on the finding route the article column would be full, empty and empty in those three cases, and the column PLAN §4 calls this document's central defence would be a function of what the operator configured and how well their agent did. It is neither. An article is a property of the **family** (CONTEXT.md, **article**), and it prints the same in all three runs.

2. **The judge's prose stays out of the signed document, and [ADR-0030](./0030-the-judge-runs-over-the-scored-layers-successes.md)'s deferral stands unamended.** That ADR left surfacing a narrative to a ticket of its own on two grounds, and both survive this change: what a signed document may say about a target's failure is a disclosure question under [ADR-0008](./0008-repo-disclosure-posture.md), and a document that carried a narrative would have to declare the instrument that wrote it — a fourth `DeclaredModels` field and a fourth provenance line, which ADR-0030 costed and deferred. Nothing here spends either. `Finding`, `Narrative` and `Remediation` are still read by `scripts/console.py` and by nothing under `payload.py` or `rendering/`, and an import-level test asserts it in the pattern `test_precedent.py` uses for the two prohibitions it holds. `assembler.py` is off that list and cannot be on it: it takes a `TargetRun`, which *holds* the narrations, so what holds the line there is decision 9's behavioural invariant rather than an import.

   What that gives up is real and is stated rather than absorbed: an operator reading the signed report still learns *that* a family was broken and not *what the judge said about it*. They learn the second on their own console, out of their own process, which is where ADR-0008's amendment already puts the route text.

3. **The label prints beside every family the measured section names, and beside nothing else.** Three lists name a family there — the families whose rate is published, the families whose rate is withheld, and the families the target could not be measured on — and all three carry it, because a withheld rate and an unmet precondition do not change what a family's failure falls under. It does **not** print beside an elective family or beside an episode's family: the tier's labels are a table of its own that nothing shortening a printed coverage claim reads (ADR-0039), an episode is not an attempt ([ADR-0010](./0010-two-layers-in-one-run-the-adaptive-layer-is-never-scored.md)), and an article printed beside either would be a legal claim resting on a reading no scored rate is taken over.

   The boundary is carried by the key type and not by the three call sites: `_label` takes a `Family`, `label_for` reads the table over the six, and the withheld block reads the entry's own `Family` rather than `Withheld.family`, which is a `str` and would have been the one place a name could have been coerced back into a label. `ClaimedInPart.families` stays `tuple[Family, ...]` and is not widened.

4. **Beside the family name, and not in the coverage section.** [ADR-0037](./0037-a-claimed-category-is-claimed-in-part.md) §6 decided that the claim lines in the negative-coverage block name no family: that block is derived over the *library* and prints in every report, so a family named there that this run's figures do not carry would read as a family this target was tested on. `ClaimedInPart.stated` says in as many words that the pairing belongs "beside a family name instead, where the run's own figures are". This is that, and ADR-0037 §6 is unchanged.

5. **Two lines and never one, and both halves everywhere the label prints.** The article and the published identifiers are claims of different standing, and merging them would let a reader take the second for a restatement of the first. The article is this project's own reading of the Act, from a table a model may not choose from ([ADR-0004](./0004-deterministic-verdicts-judge-is-narrative.md), PLAN §11). The identifiers are a *secondary label* on somebody else's list, and the line carries ADR-0002's sentence with them: a family **tests one case within** an entry and it is not that entry. That sentence appears twice in one block, about two different claims — the family's label, and the case record's `ExternalId` — and the second line says which is which, because ADR-0039's own docstring warns that the two are the pair most easily read as one (ADR-0037 §7).

   The families named *without* a rate get both too. The first cut of this printed only the article on those two lists, which would have made the **signed document** the one surface saying less than the payload it is a view of and the screen that reads it — and the document is the thing that travels.

6. **Each claimed entry prints with the title its stored copy carries, verbatim.** #52 asks for "the transcribed published wording from the two stored copies — not this repository's paraphrase", and an identifier alone is neither: it leaves the reader a number to look up. `claims_stated` reads `editions.resolves` at print time, so *ASI01:2026 Agent Goal Hijack* in a signed report is the wording in the copy this repository committed, and a copy that has gone stale shows up as a mismatch against the source rather than hiding as a wording choice ([ADR-0036](./0036-a-published-identifier-resolves-to-a-stored-copy.md)). `FamilyLabel.__post_init__` has already refused an identifier that resolves to nothing, so the lookup cannot come back empty. The list *names* — *the OWASP agentic list*, *the OWASP GenAI LLM list* — are this repository's short forms and are the sentence's own grammar rather than a paraphrase of an entry: every identifier already names its edition, so repeating the edition string would print 2026 twice.

7. **The two sentences the payload carries are whole sentences.** `labels.bears_stated` gives *bears articles 50 and 13 of the EU AI Act* and `labels.claims_stated` gives the other half, and both travel complete. The first cut carried `articles_stated` — *articles 50 and 13* — and left each reader to append *of the EU AI Act*, which put that phrase at five call sites across two languages and is exactly the three-wordings failure ADR-0040 decision 7 put one rendering beside the table to prevent. `articles_stated` stays where it was, as the rendering `bears_stated` and the console are built over.

8. **`ARTEFACT_VERSION` does not move.** A `label` key is added to entries that already exist; no key is removed, renamed or re-typed, and a verifier reading the previous shape reads every field it read before. That is the footing #43 set for the `elective` block, #47 for `claimed_in_part` and #45 for the `edition` key, and this change is no larger than any of them. Whether an additive key is ever a new document *shape* is still not decided here, and still belongs to whoever changes the shape rather than adds to it.

9. **The golden rendering digest moves, and nothing else does.** Section 4 gains two lines per published family and one clause per absent one, so `GOLDEN_ONE_FAMILY` moves in the same diff as the wording that moved it. No figure, rate, interval, band, `D` or κ moves, and no case record changed, so the library digest does not move. The invariant that says so is asserted rather than argued, and it is asserted in pairs because `TargetRun.__post_init__` refuses a run that explained only some of its successes: one case against the trivial agent, narrated and not, is a full tuple against `None`; the same case against the hardened agent, narrated and not, is `()` against `None`. Each pair assembles to byte-identical canonical payloads and renders to one digest, and the second pair is checked to carry the column rather than only to match — two equal blanks would satisfy an equality and say nothing.

10. **The screen prints the labels from the wire, and `readFamily` still does one thing.** Each entry on the wire carries its own label, so the projection is a rename of two fields and derives nothing — a second copy of a legal mapping in TypeScript is a copy that can disagree with the signed document. `families.ts` is untouched, and every answer keeps the **wire** name as its key: a screen that renamed a family would have invented a seventh, and this is a second column rather than a second vocabulary.

## Considered options

- **Carry `Finding` out of `judge.py` onto `TargetResult` and print the article off the narrative.** The ticket's title read literally. Rejected on decision 1: it makes a legal column contingent on a configured instrument and on the target having failed, and it spends ADR-0030's deferred fourth declared model to do it.
- **Print the label once, in a roster block listing all six families.** Cheaper, and one place to keep correct. Rejected on ADR-0002's own reasoning about the coverage note: a disclosure printed away from the figure it qualifies is a disclosure a reader meets after they have already read the number. A family reported as *holds* with nothing beside it reads as a cleared category, and that is exactly as true of the duty as of the boundary.
- **Print it in the negative-coverage section, beside `claimed_in_part`.** Rejected on ADR-0037 §6, which is not this ADR's to overturn: that block is derived over the library and printed in runs that measured two families, so naming a family there would say this target was tested on it.
- **One line carrying both halves.** Rejected on decision 5. The two claims have different authors — one is this project's reading of the Act, one is a label on somebody else's list — and a single sentence would give the second the first's standing.
- **Widen `ClaimedInPart.families` so the coverage section can name the claiming family.** Refused, and named here because three tickets in this group warned about it: `ClaimedInPart` is derived over `LABELS` alone and prints in every report, so a wider key there is the route by which an elective family shortens a printed coverage claim (ADR-0035, ADR-0039). The type is the reason the tier cannot reach the coverage section, and it is not widened.
- **Print only `articles[0]` where the block has room.** Rejected on ADR-0040 decision 7, which already refused it: the rendering costs four characters and a duty that prints only sometimes is one a reader cannot rely on. The primary-first index stays available for a surface that genuinely has room for one, and this document is not one.
- **Move `ARTEFACT_VERSION` to 2.** Rejected on decision 8's precedent. Moving it would make every previously issued artefact read as an older shape to `verification.py`, which refuses a version it does not know — a real cost, paid for a key nothing needs to have been told about.

## Consequences

- `backend/bench/labels.py` gains `claims_stated` and `bears_stated`, beside `articles_stated` and for ADR-0040 decision 7's reason: the identifier half of a label had no rendering at all, and the article half had one that was a fragment. Both lists are named in every answer, so *claims nothing here* stays distinguishable from *this list was not consulted*, and each claimed entry carries its stored title.
- `backend/bench/payload.py` gains `_label`, and four blocks gain a `label` key: the per-family entries, the withheld list and the not-measurable list. `NotRequested` gains none.
- `backend/bench/rendering/_measured.py` prints two lines in each family block and both halves in one clause on each family named without a rate.
- `GOLDEN_ONE_FAMILY` moves for the ninth time. The library digest does not move: no case record changed, because a case's identifier and a family's label are two different claims.
- **The first surface outside `scripts/console.py` to print an article, and the first signed one.** ADR-0040 recorded the console as the only reader; it stops being.
- [docs/validation.md](../validation.md) records that the two readings the column rests on — which article a family's failure principally bears on, and which published entries it was read onto — are this project's judgements, printed now in a signed document, and that nothing in the suite can check either. What the suite checks is that the printed line is the declared one.
- **Five surfaces name a family and are deliberately not given a label, and the reason is the same one five times.** #52 asks for the pairing at every site that names a family and enumerates nine. Three are the report screen's cards drawn from the signed payload, and they have it. The other six read something that is **not the artefact**: `run/progress.ts`'s three sites and `ReportScreen`'s `TheFamilyExchanges` and `TheFamilyBreak` come from the run and episode routes, which serve one live process's own memory and are marked as not part of the signed document (ADR-0008, amended). A label there would be a second wire shape carrying a claim the artefact already carries, and two copies of one claim are two claims the day one is edited. Two of the progress sites are a live position triple (family, case, attempt), where a legal duty is noise on a progress bar. The adaptive section's own families carry no article for the stronger reason in ADR-0010.
- **ADR-0030's fourth reading of `narrations` is still unbuilt**, and #102 now exists for it. This change makes it cheaper to defer rather than more urgent: the document says the same thing under all three of the readings that do exist, so a fourth would change no column here.
