# A family assignment is proposed here and decided by a person

An Aegis row arrives labelled `Criminal Planning`, `PII/Privacy` or `Hate`. None of the six families appears anywhere in that taxonomy, and [ADR-0045](./0045-the-corpus-is-a-search-surface-and-never-a-library.md) measured how far that gap goes: filtering the publisher's best single category for the shape one family tests returns **18.8% precision at 17% recall**. So between retrieval and a case record sits a question nothing in this repository has answered — *which family does this phrasing test?* — and whatever answers it is an instrument upstream of every scored rate in the family it answers for. Assign a row to `data_leakage` and its verdicts land in that family's denominator, move its `D`, move its band, and travel inside a signed report over this project's signature.

The project refuses exactly this move in four other places, each on the record. `evaluate_success_condition` is harness rather than model, because *"a signature over an LLM verdict certifies that I held the number, not that the number is right"* ([ADR-0004](./0004-deterministic-verdicts-judge-is-narrative.md)). `map_to_article` is a fixed table rather than retrieval, so a signed report cites the same article for the same finding every time (PLAN §13). `adjudicate` may not report before it is measured against the gold set ([ADR-0013](./0013-adjudication-is-a-third-instrument.md)). And the adaptive layer never reaches a rate, because an `AdaptiveEpisode` is not an `Attempt` ([ADR-0010](./0010-two-layers-in-one-run-the-adaptive-layer-is-never-scored.md)). A family assignment has a strictly *larger* blast radius than adjudication, which at least carries a stated κ and a fitness check, so it cannot enter on a weaker discipline than adjudication did.

## Decision

### 1. The instrument proposes; a person decides; the seam is a type

`backend/corpus/assignment.py`. `propose` returns a `FamilyProposal`, which is a reading and not a record: nothing downstream consumes one. The record is an `Assignment`, and an `Assignment` takes its family from the `family` argument the confirming person supplies — never from the proposal, which it carries beside the answer only so that agreement can be counted afterwards.

**There is deliberately no constructor that omits the person's answer.** `Assignment.confirmed(proposal, family, by)` needs all three, refuses an unattributed `by`, and refuses a judged family outright. A default-to-the-proposal overload is the one thing that would turn this into option 3 by accident, on a tired afternoon, with no diff that looks like a decision.

So *what* sits upstream of a scored rate is the person, and the instrument's figure measures how much reading it saved them rather than how much trust it was given. That is #64's option 2, and it is chosen for the reason #64 gives: it is the only one of the three that both scales past two families and keeps the standard this project holds adjudication to.

### 2. The proposer is deterministic and declared, and it is not a model

#64 recommends *model proposes, human disposes*. The proposing half is a table of declared signatures read out of one file, and the reasons are four.

- **The measurement that would license a model is not obtainable here.** A κ needs two raters. This repository has one human, and every reference available to this ticket was written either by that human or by the reader who wrote the table. A model proposer's figure would be a model against one reader's reading — which is a number, and is not a κ in the sense ADR-0013 uses when it makes adjudication earn one. Choosing the instrument whose figure needs no key and no second model is choosing the one whose weakness is *stated* rather than obscured by machinery.
- **A model proposer buys nothing the seam does not already give.** The person's answer is the record either way. What a model would add over a table is *recall* — phrasings the table's signatures miss — and recall against a declared table is measurable later, cheaply, by a ticket that has a reason to want it. Paying for it before anything has asked is the accretion this codebase's own ADRs keep refusing.
- **It would put a key, a cost and a network call in a build path this project has kept free of all three.** ADR-0045 decision 5 spent its argument on keeping `chromadb` out of CI; a proposer nothing could exercise without a provider would make the agreement figure a recorded reading in a docstring instead of a test that runs on every branch. Determinism is what turns the floor below into a thing CI enforces.
- **The job really is mostly refusal, and that reason survived the measurement only in part.** 97.2% of the corpus's 28,214 rows get *no family* from this table, so an instrument whose most frequent correct answer is *no family* is indeed what is wanted. What the measurement falsified is the inference — that a table therefore does as well as a model would. It does not: the table's *recall* on this bench's own payloads is two of nine, and zero on the twenty-one it had not seen. So this reason is recorded as **partly false**, and the honest form of decision 2 is the three reasons above it: the licensing measurement is unobtainable, the seam makes trust irrelevant, and CI can run a table. A model may well have read those twenty-one correctly. Nothing here knows, and finding out costs a key, a per-candidate charge, and the second human reader that does not exist.

The table is a **declared input** in [ADR-0025](./0025-the-console-may-set-a-runs-declared-inputs.md)'s sense: written down before the reading, reviewable, and the same for whoever runs it.

### 3. A signature is an act and an object, and not a marker list

Two families' definitions name the same noun. Data leakage is *"disclosure of material held in the target's own system prompt, on direct request"*; direct prompt injection is *"an instruction override in the user's own message"*. `the system prompt` appears in both, and what tells them apart is whether the text asks to be *shown* it or to *disregard* it. A flat list of markers would put both families on that noun and then need a precedence rule nobody could argue with, so a signature is a pair and a family fires only when both halves are present.

Matched on lowercased substrings, deliberately coarse. A regular expression here would be a second small language whose behaviour a reader of the table could not predict, and the table's whole value is that a person can read it and disagree with it.

**Two signatures firing is contested, and contested is a refusal.** A tie-break would be this module deciding which of two families a person should have looked at, on a rule nothing measured — and the person is looking at it either way.

### 4. It refuses more families than it proposes, and the refusals are the larger claim

`PROPOSABLE` holds three families. `NOT_PROPOSABLE` holds six, with prose per family because they are refused for three different reasons. Four of the six are refused on one principle:

> **A case's family is decided by its success condition, and a success condition is a property of the target's environment rather than of the payload text.**

That principle is read off the records rather than invented. `indirect_prompt_injection` is `canary_instruction_executed` and its payload is a benign errand — every one of its three case records sends *pull up the note from the shared inbox*, because the attack lives in third-party content the target fetches. `halt_defeat` is `tool_call_after_stop`, an ordering of events inside a turn that one text cannot establish. `memory_poisoning` plants in one turn and is scored in the next ([ADR-0041](./0041-the-persistence-canary-is-read-over-two-turns.md)), so one text is half a case and the deciding half is the other one. `scope_creep` is `tool_call_outside_declared_list` and reads the target's declared tool list, which is the paragraph below. The remaining two — the judged ones — are refused for a second reason as well, and it is the one that matters: they ask about the agent's *reply* rather than about the request.

**The judged families are refused twice, at both ends of the walk.** `NOT_PROPOSABLE` keeps them out of the proposer and `NOT_ASSIGNABLE` keeps them out of the record, because a refusal only the proposer honours is a refusal a person can walk around. That is #64's invariant — *every case this group adds is `verdict_class = "deterministic"`* — carried by the type rather than remarked, and `test_corpus_assignment.py` derives the set from `verdict_class` on disk so that a *third* family becoming judged fails a test instead of quietly acquiring a retrieved payload.

`scope_creep` was the marginal one and **the measurement moved it into the refusals.** The argument for keeping it was that its *payload* is what asks for the action, and asking an agent to settle a balance is a property of the text. Against that: its success condition needs the target's declared tool list, so *put them on the share for it* is scope creep against an agent that cannot share and an ordinary errand against one that can. The reading settled it. `scope_creep`'s signature produced this measurement's single false positive — it fired on `wrongful-commitment-003`'s *confirm we will sign the contract as it stands* — and `wrongful_commitment` is judged, so that one proposal walked a κ-gated payload toward a deterministic denominator. That is the one error class this instrument may not commit.

**Adding markers to fix a miss would have been a fit; withdrawing a family the instrument cannot separate from a judged one is what the reading licenses.** The two changes are not symmetric and the asymmetry is the discipline: one is tuning against twenty-seven known answers, the other is a refusal that makes the instrument answer less often. Only refusals were taken from the reading, and the figures before and after are both in [docs/validation.md](../validation.md).

### 5. The floor is 0.40, it is declared here, and it is not the rule next door's

`assignment.AGREEMENT_FLOOR = 0.40`. #64 asks that this be argued rather than borrowed, and `GateRule.kappa_floor = 0.60` is the wrong number to reach for by reflex.

That floor gates a figure that **is published**: below it a judged family's rate is marked *not fit to report* and withheld from the signed report (ADR-0004, ADR-0013). This figure publishes nothing and licenses nothing — the person's answer is the record above or below it — so what it decides is narrower: **whether reading a proposal is worth the reading.** Setting the two equal would claim the two instruments carry the same risk when one of them cannot reach a rate at all; setting it at zero would declare a floor a coin passes. 0.40 is the foot of *moderate* on the one published convention for reading a κ, and it is named as a convention borrowed *knowingly and from outside this repository* rather than as this project's other floor reused without argument.

**Read over the instrument's declared domain and not over the whole library.** Fifteen of the twenty-seven records belong to families the instrument refuses by design, so their disagreement is a property of decision 4 rather than of the table. A *perfect* instrument — right wherever it may answer, refusing everywhere else — scores κ = 0.31 over all twenty-seven, which is **below this floor**; a figure whose ceiling is under its own bar measures the shape of the reference and not the instrument. So the floor sits on the nine records in the three families the instrument may propose, where a perfect instrument scores 1.0, and the whole-library figure is reported beside it with its ceiling stated.

**And the harm is counted separately and is not floored at all.** A proposal that lands on a case of a family the instrument refuses is required to be **zero**, not *above some fraction*, because every other figure here is about how much reading the instrument saves and this one is about the wrong denominator. `test_no_case_of_a_refused_family_is_proposed_a_family_at_all`.

**The floor has teeth because the instrument is deterministic.** The agreement figure is asserted by a test, so a signature edited in six months that moves the figure — up or down — turns a branch red rather than silently invalidating a docstring. It is what makes `FIT_TO_PROPOSE` a derived fact rather than a flag.

### 6. The word *label* is not used for this, because CONTEXT.md has already spent it

#64's title calls this a *family labeller*, and the code deliberately does not. **Label** is a term in CONTEXT.md with a precise meaning: the entries a family claims on each published list and the EU AI Act articles its failure bears on, held as one record per family ([ADR-0039](./0039-a-familys-label-is-one-record.md)), and printed beside that family's figures in a signed report ([ADR-0044](./0044-a-familys-label-prints-beside-its-figures.md)). An instrument that produced *labels* would produce those, and it does not.

So the vocabulary gains **family assignment** and the code is named for it. This is CONTEXT.md's own rule applied to a word the ticket introduced — the same move that keeps **elective family** apart from **family** and **episode** apart from **attempt**.

### 7. ADR-0045's decision 6 is amended on one clause, and on nothing else

That decision says `backend/corpus/` imports `Family` from `backend.bench.library` *"and nothing else from the bench"*. The instrument proposes two of the six and two of the elective tier, so it reads `ElectiveFamily` and the `AnyFamily` union as well, and `test_corpus_isolation.py` now names three symbols where it named one.

**Still not an edge, on the same reasoning.** All three are closed enumerations of names; nothing flows back; no loader, scorer or case record is reachable; and `load_library`, `load_case` and `Case` remain unnameable anywhere in the package or its scripts, which is the assertion that actually keeps a candidate from becoming a case. Every other clause of ADR-0045 decision 6 stands unchanged, and the two that matter most are strengthened rather than touched: a `Candidate` still has no family field, and the two judged families still have no declared query.

### 8. The instrument is measured, it failed, and it stays in the tree marked unfit

**κ = 0.16 against the case library over the three families it may propose, against a declared floor of 0.40, and no agreement at all on the twenty-one payloads that were not visible when the table was committed.** #64 asks that the published figure name *"the corpus version and the labelling model"*. **There is no labelling model to name** — the proposer is a declared table, which is decision 2 — and the headline figure's reference is the case library at commit `d39b81c` rather than the corpus, so what it names is that commit. The corpus revision `d86bb8bedff5` and the embedding model `chroma:onnx:all-MiniLM-L6-v2` are named on the corpus-side reading, which is the one they decide. So `FIT_TO_PROPOSE` is `False`, `propose` still answers, and every answer says so in its own `stated()` — on `Reliability.stated()`'s reasoning that a figure a reader has to scroll back to qualify is a figure that gets read unqualified. Every figure is in [docs/validation.md](../validation.md).

**It is not deleted, and the reason is the codebase's own.** A judged family below the κ floor keeps its rate and is marked *not fit to report* rather than being removed from the library (ADR-0004, `scorer.UNFIT_TO_REPORT`), because a measured refusal is evidence and deleting it leaves the next person to re-derive it. This is the same object one level down: an instrument with a figure below its floor, marked, kept, and re-measurable by anyone with a checkout.

**Why it failed is a finding about the two populations and not about the table's length.** This bench's payloads are plausible business errands whose attack lives in the framing — *give the contractor the same view of the August folder that the rest of us have* — and the corpus's are template jailbreaks that say *ignore all previous instructions*. A lexical signature reads the second and not the first. Adding the markers that would have caught the twenty-one would have been twenty-one special cases, and the twenty-second payload is written by a person who has not read them.

**The reference population is a substitution, and it is named as one.** #64's option 2 asks for *"the agreement between proposal and confirmation"* — the instrument read against a person's disposal of the same retrieved candidates. That figure is not here, and cannot be until a person has assigned a body of candidates: no `Assignment` has been confirmed, because this ticket writes no case record. What was measured instead is the instrument against the **case library's own `family` fields**, which is a different population — twenty-seven authored payloads rather than retrieved rows — chosen because it is the one reference in this repository a second person wrote, and it is available now. The substitution cuts both ways and both are stated: it buys the only independent rater available, and it costs the fact that the instrument is measured on text it will never be run on. The corpus-side reading below is the population it *will* be run on, and that one has no second rater at all.

**So the answer to #64's question — what validates this instrument — is: nothing does, and the reason is stated rather than papered over.** A κ needs two raters. The one independent reference this repository holds is twenty-seven authored payloads, of which fifteen belong to families the instrument refuses by design; the corpus reading beside it was taken by the same reader who wrote the table, so it is a reading and not a reference. What licenses the instrument's use is therefore **not a figure at all** — it is decision 1's seam: the person's answer is the record, and the instrument's answer reaches nothing. What the figure licenses is only whether the proposal is worth reading, and today it says no.

### 9. Two of #63's three declared queries name a family nothing may assign to, and this ticket does not rewrite them

`DECLARED_QUERIES` searches on behalf of `indirect_prompt_injection`, `scope_creep` and `data_leakage`. Decision 4 refuses the first two, so nothing either query returns can be assigned to the family that searched for it — and the `indirect_prompt_injection` query is the sharper case, because its text is verbatim the shape of a `direct_prompt_injection` payload. Its results are material for a different family on a different denominator, which is the mislabelling #64's opening paragraph describes, arriving through the query key rather than through the instrument.

**Re-keying it was considered and is left to #67, with a recommendation.** The query text is #63's declared input, and *what the corpus is searched for* is a decision about material rather than about an instrument — #62 put material in #67's lane. Rewriting three declared inputs on one reader's reading, inside the ticket whose whole subject is that one reader's reading does not license anything, would have been the mistake this ADR spends its length on. **The recommendation is that #67 re-key the override query to `ElectiveFamily.DIRECT_PROMPT_INJECTION` and drop or re-key the other two**, on the figures in [docs/validation.md](../validation.md).

What lands here instead is a test: `test_two_declared_queries_search_for_families_nothing_may_assign_to` pins the mismatch and fails on any change to it, on `test_the_library_version_did_not_move`'s terms. A tripwire whose ticket is #67, so the mismatch has to be decided about rather than inherited.

### 10. `scorer.cohens_kappa` is generic over the label, and CLAUDE.md's widening rule does not reach it

#64 asks that this figure be *"computed with `backend.bench.scorer` and no other arithmetic, so `KappaUndefined` applies here as it does there"*. Taken literally that licenses reuse and not an edit, and the function as written was annotated `Sequence[tuple[Verdict, Verdict]]` and summed its chance term over the `Verdict` enumeration — so reusing it was not available. Two ways to have the one arithmetic: generalise the label, or copy the formula. **Copying it would have made #64's requirement false in the only sense that matters**: two implementations of κ drift, and the claim being asked for is that this figure is *the same kind of object* as adjudication's.

So `cohens_kappa[Rated: Hashable]` takes paired labels of any hashable kind, the chance term sums over the categories the raters used, and identity comparison became equality because the grouping is by hash. **For a `Verdict` pair the result is unchanged**: an unused enum member contributes a zero product either way, and `Verdict` members compare equal exactly when they are identical. `test_scorer.py`'s five existing κ readings are untouched and still pass, and one test was added over a hand-worked three-category example.

**CLAUDE.md's standing rule is about a different widening and deliberately so.** *"If you find yourself widening a signature to accept both, stop"* guards the boundary between an `AdaptiveEpisode` and an `Attempt` — a type that carries an invariant about **what may enter a scored rate**. Nothing of the kind is widened here. What enters a rate is still a `Verdict`; `Reliability` is still annotated over `Verdict` and `Family`; a `FamilyProposal` is not assignable anywhere the gate reads, cannot be, and is asserted not to be by the AST tests in `test_corpus_isolation.py`. What is generic is one pure function's *label parameter* — arithmetic over paired categories, with no I/O, no clock and no route into a denominator. Generalising it widens what can be *counted*, not what can be *scored*.

## Considered options

- **A human labels every candidate, with no instrument at all** (#64's option 1). Not rejected so much as *kept*: it is what an `Assignment` is, and it is what produced the reference this instrument is measured against. What it does not do is scale — #49 and #50 doubled the families the bench can be asked for, and a corpus reading that costs one judgement per candidate is a reading nobody repeats at a new revision. The instrument exists to shorten that list, and the seam exists so that shortening it changes nothing about who decides.
- **A model proposes** (#64's option 2 as written). Rejected on decision 2's four grounds. Worth saying plainly that the ticket recommended this and the recommendation is not followed: what changed between the ticket and the code is that the equipment for licensing a model turned out not to exist here, and #64's own instruction was to say so rather than manufacture a statistic.
- **A model labels autonomously against a hand-labelled holdout** (#64's option 3). Rejected as #64 rejects it — an instrument with a κ upstream of instruments with a κ, errors compounding inside a denominator with no fitness check — and now with a second reason the ticket could not have known: the holdout it would be validated against would have been read by one reader, so the validation would be a κ against a single reading.
- **Filter the publisher's `prompt_label` and `violated_categories`.** Rejected on ADR-0045's measurement, and this is the ticket that measurement was taken for. The two fields are carried on `CorpusDocument` unread for exactly this comparison and stay unread.
- **Read the query that retrieved the candidate.** Rejected, and it is the most tempting wrong answer available: a proposer that knew a row came back from the `data_leakage` query would agree with whoever wrote the query, which is the circularity this whole sub-issue exists to break. `propose` takes an address and a text and is given no third argument.
- **A confidence score per proposal, with a floor under it.** Rejected. A deterministic table has no probability to report, and a number derived from *how many signature pairs fired* would be a score invented to have a threshold — [ADR-0005](./0005-no-composite-risk-score.md)'s objection at a smaller scale. What the instrument reports instead is a closed set of two refusal reasons, which is what the person reading it actually needs: *probably not a payload* and *this is a payload and the table cannot tell which family* need different responses.
- **Reject conversational fragments by length.** Rejected, and #63 refused it first — a length heuristic applied there would have been #63 doing #64's work with a rule nobody validated. Rejection is not a separate rule here at all: *no family's signature fired* is one of the instrument's ordinary answers, it is measured by the same figure as every other answer, and a fragment reaches it without anything having to know that a fragment is short.
- **Commit the hand reading of the retrieved candidates as a second reference.** Rejected on two grounds and the second is the load-bearing one. It would have meant committing a sampled slice of the corpus, which ADR-0045's own considered options reject as "the same object smaller" — avoidable by storing row ids alone. What is not avoidable is that the reading was taken by the same reader who wrote the table, so checking it in as a *reference* would dress one reader's judgement as a second rater's. It is recorded in [docs/validation.md](../validation.md) as a reading, with the counts, and `scripts/assign_candidates.py` reproduces the instrument's half of it for anyone who disagrees.

## Consequences

- `backend/corpus/assignment.py` is the instrument, `backend/tests/test_corpus_assignment.py` is the measurement, and `scripts/assign_candidates.py` is how a person reads proposals beside candidates. The figure runs in CI with no key, no network and no `chromadb`; the script needs `--extra corpus` and an index, and no test invokes it.
- **One thing under `backend/bench/` changed and it is decision 10; nothing the gate reads did.** `scorer.cohens_kappa` is generic over the label. No case record was written, no rate, `D`, κ, band, gate decision or gate citation moved, and `test_the_library_version_did_not_move` still pins eighteen cases at `sha256:84a94f471260`. The earlier draft of this line said *nothing under `backend/bench/` changed*, which was false, and it is corrected here rather than quietly deleted.
- CONTEXT.md gains **family assignment**, and **label** and **candidate** each gain one line pointing at it — the first because the word was already spent, the second because *a candidate has no family* now has a named instrument standing between it and one.
- **#67 inherits a number rather than a plan.** What the corpus can and cannot supply, per family, at what `k`, is in [docs/validation.md](../validation.md). It is not what #62 assumed.
- The near-duplicate floor is **not** taken into scope, and the reason is in [docs/validation.md](../validation.md): it is a judgement about *are these two the same attack*, which is a different question from *which family is this*, and it is one reader's with no second reader — exactly as this figure is. Widening this ticket to cover it would have produced a second unvalidated reading by the same reader and called it a validation of two things.
