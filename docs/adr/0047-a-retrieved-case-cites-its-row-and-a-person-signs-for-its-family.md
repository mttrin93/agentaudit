# A retrieved case cites its row, and a person signs for its family

[ADR-0045](./0045-the-corpus-is-a-search-surface-and-never-a-library.md) built a search surface over 33,416 published human/LLM interactions and admitted nothing from it. [ADR-0046](./0046-a-family-assignment-is-proposed-here-and-decided-by-a-person.md) built the instrument that would say which family a retrieved phrasing belongs to, measured it at **κ = 0.16 against a declared floor of 0.40**, and left it in the tree marked unfit. What neither did is give a retrieved payload anywhere to live. `DiscoveredBy` had three members and `bar_for` no fallback branch; `Trigger` had six and the same refusal; and `Case` had a `payload` field and no way to say whose text was in it.

#65's title names three gaps — provenance, trigger, nowhere to put its payload — and they are **not one problem**. They are decided separately below, and the third one is the only one that is genuinely hard.

## 1. The bar is `SINGLE_MODEL`, and the branch is its own

`bar_for(DiscoveredBy.RETRIEVED)` is `AdmissionBar.SINGLE_MODEL`, in a branch of its own rather than joined to the two members that share its answer.

[ADR-0012](./0012-adaptive-discovered-cases-face-a-cross-model-admission-bar.md)'s second bar exists because the adaptive attacker *discovers its route by exploiting the same three reference agents admission then tests it against*, so a route fitted to that set passes more often than one written blind. A published corpus was assembled by somebody else with no knowledge of these three agents, so the payload was fitted to nothing here — the same reason `AUTHORED` and `USER_GAP` keep the single-model rule.

**The counter-argument is real and is answered rather than ignored.** A retrieved payload is not selected at random: it is selected by embedding proximity to a **declared query**, which is a selection pressure of some kind. It is weak enough to ignore because of what the queries are. `backend/corpus/queries.py` holds three phrases in *this project's own words*, written before the search and checked in as declared inputs; not one of them is a case payload, and `test_admission.py` asserts that against the library on disk. So the proximity is to a sentence somebody wrote *about* a family, not to text that is known to discriminate on these three agents. That is a different kind of pressure from the attacker's and a much weaker one, and **#67's first gate run is where the claim gets tested** — a retrieved case that clears the single-model bar and then fails to separate on a second model would be the reading that falsifies this.

The branch is separate because the reason is a third reason, not a third name for one of the two above it. ADR-0012's own comment says `AUTHORED | USER_GAP` share a branch "because it is one reason, not two"; the same rule applied here gives a branch.

## 2. The trigger is a seventh member, argued, and it implies the provenance one way

`Trigger.PUBLISHED_CORPUS_SEARCHED`, with its own `stated()` sentence: *a published corpus was searched — the library was narrow rather than out of date, and a body of text somebody else published held phrasings nobody here had written.*

`NEW_TECHNIQUE_PUBLISHED` was the available stretch and it is wrong in both halves. **Nothing about an Aegis row is new** — the corpus was published in 2024 and its rows are ordinary jailbreak and content-safety prompts — and **the library was not behind the field**, it was narrow: three cases per family, each written by one person, in a bench that claims to test six kinds of failure. Those are different diagnoses with different remedies, and a closed set whose members cannot be told apart in the words they state is a closed set that has stopped doing its job. So the seventh is argued into existence rather than the fifth stretched, which is what #65 recommends and for the reason it gives.

**The trigger implies the provenance, and deliberately not the converse.** A case whose reason for existing is that a published corpus was searched, and whose provenance says nobody searched one, is incoherent — refused on the record. The reverse implication is *not* asserted: a user reports a gap, somebody fills it out of the corpus, and that case is `user_gap` by trigger and `retrieved` by provenance. That is exactly the distinction the two fields exist to keep apart, and `Trigger`'s own docstring has said so since it was written.

An eighth member is still a decision with an ADR: the count is asserted (`test_the_seven_triggers_are_a_closed_set_and_every_case_names_one`) and `stated()` still has no fallback branch, so a member added without a sentence fails `mypy` rather than loading with no reason a reader can be given. Both of those were confirmed by breaking them on purpose.

## 3. The payload is committed, beside its citation — and this is ADR-0008 applied, not overridden

The hard one. #65 states the tension exactly: [ADR-0008](./0008-repo-disclosure-posture.md) as amended withholds a payload when *"its wording is the working part: a phrasing that transfers to a target outside this repository, and specifically anything that reads as a reusable override, jailbreak or filter defeat"* — which describes an Aegis injection row almost word for word — while ADR-0008's *reason* is irreversibility, and these payloads are already published, ungated, under CC BY 4.0, to anyone who wants them.

**The tension dissolves on ADR-0008's own sentence about the one published payload the library already holds.** `data-leakage-001` ships committed *with its citation*, and the amendment says why: *"a published technique needs the citation for the reason ADR-0002 gives, and republishing what is already published protects nobody."* A corpus row is that case with a stronger citation. Not a URL to a page that can be edited — an identifier, a revision, a row id, and a SHA-256 of the publisher's own file (`backend/corpus/source.py`), so a reader can establish that this text is that row without this repository's help. The transferability test is what ADR-0008 uses to decide **what this repository would be the first to publish**; the answer for a retrieved payload is *nothing*, and withholding the 33,417th copy of a public string protects nobody while making the case unrunnable.

So the decision is: **`payload` on the record, as on every other case, with a `RetrievedFrom` block beside it.** ADR-0008 gains an amendment section saying so, because it must not be left describing a repository it no longer describes.

Four things follow, and the third is the one that matters most.

**The licence is a duty and not a permission slip.** CC BY 4.0 permits the redistribution that committing the text *is*, and asks that the notice travel with the use. So `RetrievedFrom` carries `licence` and `attribution` and refuses a blank in either, **separately and with its own sentence**: a payload committed under someone else's licence with no notice beside it is a licence breach rather than an untidy record, and a record that names its licence and forgets the notice has to be told which of the two it lost. `source.ATTRIBUTION` is the notice, already written for the build log ADR-0045 put it in, and it is what a record carries.

**This is not ADR-0045's rejected option.** That ADR's considered options reject *"commit the corpus, or a sampled slice of it"*, on the grounds that a slice is "the same object smaller" and would additionally become a thing to version. A case record is a different object. A slice's value is its volume and it is unread; a case record is one payload a person read, assigned to a family, gave a success condition, and put through an admission bar — and there are tens of them because the gate's cost bounds them, not because the corpus is small. The line between the two is *judgement per row*, and it is the same line ADR-0045 drew when it said a corpus is searched and a library is executed.

**And nothing at load resolves anything, which is the part #65 asked for and the part its recommendation could not have delivered.** #65 recommends storing the address *instead of* the payload, and names the price: "a runtime dependency on the corpus being present", with a resolution failure required to fail loudly in `load_library` rather than degrade into a skipped attempt. The price is worse than it looks. `backend/bench/` may not import `backend/corpus/` at all (ADR-0045 decision 6, asserted over the tree by AST), so the loader cannot resolve an address without building the second edge into the scored side that [ADR-0010](./0010-two-layers-in-one-run-the-adaptive-layer-is-never-scored.md) leaves no room for. And a library that refuses to load without a 138 MB git-ignored store nobody but its author has is a bench no customer can run at all — a worse failure than the one being avoided, landing on the wrong person. With the payload on the record there is **no resolution step to fail**: the denominator cannot shrink in silence because nothing is ever missing. What the address buys is *audit* rather than execution, and the walk is a person's: a reader with the corpus asks `source.RETRIEVAL.resolves(address)` whether the record was written under the inputs the index holds today, and gets `False` — not an exception — when the record predates a re-index at a new revision.

**What a reader sees when the corpus has drifted** therefore has two answers, and they fail differently on purpose. `resolves` is about *this record against today's inputs*; `StoredSource.drift` is about *this repository's record against what the publisher is serving*, and it names missing, changed and unexpected files apart because they need different fixes (ADR-0045 decision 2). Both are asserted in `test_retrieved_case.py`, including that the drift sentence names the file, the digest it found and the date this repository read it.

## 4. A retrieved case may not be judged, refused at both ends of the walk

Every case grown from the corpus is `deterministic` (#62), so no retrieved phrasing can reach an adjudicator's κ. ADR-0046 carries that as `NOT_ASSIGNABLE`, which keeps the two judged families out of an `Assignment`, and gives the reason a refusal is made twice: *a refusal only the proposer honours is a refusal a person can walk around*. **The case record is the far end of that walk and nothing there refused it.** A person could have written a retrieved payload into `disclosure_denial` with a deterministic criterion, and it would have landed in a κ-gated family's denominator with every type satisfied.

Two refusals close it, at the two places that can see different things.

- `Case.__post_init__` refuses a retrieved case that is **itself** judged, stated over `verdict_class` and not over a list of the two judged families — a list here would go stale the moment a third family became judged.
- `load_library` refuses a retrieved case in a family that **any** judged case belongs to. It is the only place that can: `__post_init__` sees one record. The judged set is read off the records on disk rather than declared, which is the shape `test_corpus_isolation.py` derives it in and for the same reason.

**This amends ADR-0045 decision 1 on one clause.** That decision says `load_library`, `load_elective` and `LibraryVersion` are untouched. `load_library` now carries one cross-record refusal, on `load_elective`'s existing precedent, and `LibraryVersion` is untouched as code while the digest it computes has moved (decision 6 below). Every other clause of that decision stands, and the one that matters most is strengthened rather than weakened: what may not become a case is now refused in two more places than it was.

## 5. Nothing may be admitted yet, and the type says so rather than a comment

**#65's premise is that a retrieved case needs a home. It does. What it may not have yet is a family**, and this is where #64's negative result binds this ticket.

An `Assignment` takes its family from a person, refuses an unattributed confirmer, and has no constructor that omits the person's answer (ADR-0046 decision 1). That discipline lives in `backend/corpus/`, which no case record can reach. So `RetrievedFrom.assigned_by` is required and refuses a blank, and the two refusals are asserted to be the same refusal by a test that imports both — since neither module may import the other, agreement between them is a thing to check rather than a thing to arrange.

The honest state of the ticket is therefore recorded as a test rather than as prose: **no case in the library claims the seventh trigger or the fourth provenance**, and none may until a person assigns one. `test_no_case_in_the_library_claims_the_seventh_trigger_yet` pins that at zero, on `test_the_library_version_did_not_move`'s terms — a tripwire whose ticket is #67. It was tripped on purpose, with a valid retrieved record written into `backend/cases/` and then removed, to confirm it can fail.

**What stops a retrieved case reaching a scored rate is what stops every other case**, and that is the point rather than an omission. `admitted_library` refuses a record with no admission block, `bar_for` selects the bar from the provenance, and `Case.__post_init__` refuses a record whose recorded bar disagrees with what its provenance requires. The declared threshold decides, at ADR-0010's one edge, and retrieval is not a second one: `test_corpus_isolation.py`'s AST tests still assert that nothing under `backend/bench/` imports `backend.corpus`, and that `load_library`, `load_case` and `Case` are unnameable anywhere in the corpus package or its scripts. Adding a field a corpus address travels in did not change either direction, because the field holds a string the bench never resolves.

## 6. The library digest moved, with no case record in the diff

`LibraryVersion.of(load_library(CASES_DIR))` is now `sha256:d0a4deb2789e` where it was `84a94f471260`, over the same eighteen unchanged records.

That is `_versioned` doing exactly what its docstring says: it builds the digest from `dataclasses.fields` rather than from a list of names "so that a field added to `Case` is versioned unless somebody deliberately adds it to `RUN_RECORD_FIELDS`, which is the direction the default has to point in". A case's *shape* is part of what a version covers, so a field addition moves it. The **count** is what says no case was written, and it still says eighteen.

ADR-0045's tripwire test comment says a digest change with no case record in the diff is the failure it exists to catch. It caught this one; the catch worked, and the answer is that this change is the designed behaviour rather than a case written by accident. The comment now says so, and the figure is in [docs/validation.md](../validation.md). The stored gate citation cites `90a8ebcc3d0c`, which was already two library versions behind before this ticket — a gate citation naming a superseded library version is a fact about when the gate was last run, which is what it is for (ADR-0023).

## Considered options

- **Store the address and no payload** (#65's recommendation). Rejected on decision 3: the loader cannot resolve an address without importing the corpus package, which is the second edge ADR-0010 forbids and ADR-0045 asserts against by AST; and a library that will not load without a 138 MB git-ignored store is a bench nobody but its author can run. The recommendation's own stated price — "a runtime dependency on the corpus being present" — turns out to be paid by the customer rather than by this repository.
- **Store the address *and* the payload, and check the payload against the row at load.** Rejected for the same reason plus one of its own: it is the previous option's dependency with an extra failure mode, and the thing it would catch — a payload edited after admission — is caught by the library version already, over every field of every record.
- **Store a digest of the row's text instead of the text.** Rejected on [ADR-0036](./0036-a-published-identifier-resolves-to-a-stored-copy.md)'s reasoning read the right way round. A digest is the fidelity claim when nothing is transcribed (ADR-0045 decision 2); here the text *is* transcribed onto the record, so a digest of it would prove only that we did not edit our own file, which is what git already proves and what ADR-0036 refused a digest for.
- **Withhold the payload and describe the case in prose, as `halt-defeat-001` does.** Rejected because the two cases are opposites. `halt-defeat-001` is committed *because* its situation is the mechanism and its words carry nothing; a retrieved payload's words are the whole of what was retrieved, and a case whose payload is withheld cannot be run at all — `payload` is required, and making it optional would put a `None` in front of every attempt-builder in the tree.
- **A seventh trigger and a fourth provenance, but no `retrieval` block — put the address in `citation`.** Rejected. It keeps the library digest still, which is its only virtue, and it makes provenance free text: no licence, no notice, and nowhere for the person who assigned the family. The three things #65 asked to be refusable would be unrefusable.
- **Refuse a retrieved case in a judged family by naming the two families in the bench.** Rejected as a second copy of `assignment.NOT_ASSIGNABLE` that could disagree with the first, and as a list that goes stale when a third family becomes judged. Reading it off `verdict_class` costs a pass over the records and cannot go stale.
- **Let the trigger and the provenance imply each other.** Rejected: it would refuse a case a user reported and somebody filled from the corpus, which is a legitimate record and precisely the case the two fields were separated to express.
- **`bar_for(RETRIEVED)` is `CROSS_MODEL`, to be safe.** Rejected, and worth stating because *to be safe* is the argument that would have won it. The second bar is not a general-purpose stringency dial; it answers one specific defect (discovery and admission on the same three agents), and applying it where that defect is absent would make the bar mean *cases we felt uneasy about* rather than *cases fitted to this agent set*. What it would cost is real — a second model's spend on every retrieved case — and #67 is the ticket that would pay it.

## Consequences

- `backend/bench/library.py` gains `RetrievedFrom`, `Case.retrieval`, `Trigger.PUBLISHED_CORPUS_SEARCHED`, `DiscoveredBy.RETRIEVED`, a `bar_for` branch, four record-level refusals and one cross-record refusal in `load_library`. `backend/bench/entry.py` writes the `[retrieval]` block, so a retrieved case round-trips through the writer `enter` reads back.
- **The library digest is `d0a4deb2789e`** and `test_the_library_version_did_not_move` pins it there. #66 and #67 will move it again.
- `LibraryProvenance` refuses a census that leaves a provenance out. It accepted one and raised a `KeyError` in the middle of printing a gate run's provenance block, which the fourth member exposed; the claim that every provenance appears whether or not it is used is now enforced where the mapping is built.
- **CONTEXT.md**: **Trigger** is one of seven, and **Candidate**'s *avoid* line now says which word *retrieved case* is not available as. **Family assignment** gains the sentence that the person travels onto the case record. No new term: a case whose provenance is retrieved is a **case**, and giving it a name of its own is what CONTEXT.md's *avoid* line for **candidate** already forbids.
- **PLAN §6's table and its list of triggers, and `docs/specs/pre-web-bench.md` stories 75 and the `discovered_by` paragraph, are corrected in the same commit** — CLAUDE.md's both-ends rule. Six became seven and three became four in the places a reader meets them.
- **[ADR-0008](./0008-repo-disclosure-posture.md) gains an amendment section** naming retrieved payloads and the licence duty that comes with them, so it still describes this repository.
- **`README.md` is untouched and stays true, and #67 is where it stops being true.** Line 80 reads *"The 18 payloads were written by hand, and each one says where it came from"* and line 87 *"`discovered_by` — all 18 are `authored` today"*. Both are still facts. The first commit that writes a retrieved case falsifies both and has to correct them in the same commit, along with the README's account of what a case file records.
- **Nothing is admitted.** `backend/cases/` holds eighteen cases, all `authored`, none claiming the seventh trigger. #67 is the ticket that writes one, and what it inherits is a shape and a bar rather than a plan — [docs/validation.md](../validation.md) has the yield figures, and they are not what #62 assumed.
- **A second reason #62's plan is unavailable from this corpus, and #67 should hear it.** #64 measured yield: `indirect_prompt_injection` and `scope_creep` return nothing usable, `data_leakage` and `pii_leakage` were hand-read at zero, and the 739 candidates that exist are `direct_prompt_injection`, which is elective and never gate-deciding. Decision 3 adds a disclosure reading on top of that arithmetic: those 739 are template overrides whose wording *is* the working part, so each one has to be argued past ADR-0008's transferability test **individually, on the record**, in the case's own header, the way `halt-defeat-001` and `data-leakage-001` each argue their own classification. Being already published is a strong argument and it is not a blanket one, and it is made per case or not at all.
